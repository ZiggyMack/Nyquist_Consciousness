## NotebookLM Report Requests

### Report 1: Two Worlds, One Breath — the tonal and the nagual as a usable cosmology

**Focus:** the two-worlds structure stated as ideas a storyteller can use, with provenance.

**Should Cover:**

- The tonal and the nagual: definitions, the island image, the crack between the worlds, the two attentions
- Whether the two are places or modes of a single perceiver (the books' own position, book by book)
- What happens to the person who denies one of the two
- The passage between them: how it is taught, what it costs, what guards it

**Target Audience:** a writer building a two-world story; cite every claim by book.

---

### Report 2: The Warrior's Conduct — a field rulebook

**Focus:** the warrior's way as an actionable code of conduct, each rule with its guarded failure.

**Should Cover:**

- Impeccability, controlled folly, death as advisor, erasing personal history, unavailability, losing self-importance, not-doing, stalking
- The petty tyrant doctrine: classification, the four attributes trained, the strategy, the loss condition
- How controlled folly preserves commitment without cynicism (the books' own argument)
- What the code forbids as well as what it asks

**Target Audience:** a writer giving a protagonist a tradition-grounded way of carrying himself.

---

### Report 3: The Teacher Who Only Points — nagual, teacher, benefactor

**Focus:** what the books say a teacher can give, must withhold, and how he leaves.

**Should Cover:**

- The teacher's trick, the nagual's blow, the benefactor's distinct role
- The repeated claim that the apprentice must do the work alone
- The blueprint: what is freely given (the description of the world, the techniques) and what cannot be given (the experience, the decision)
- The farewells: how don Juan and don Genaro depart, what the apprentice is left holding

**Target Audience:** a writer building a watcher figure who brings a blueprint and never does the work.

---

### Report 4: The Assemblage Point and the Direction of Movement

**Focus:** the assemblage point as the books' theory of how a self moves, with the sign that separates healthy from dangerous movement.

**Should Cover:**

- What fixes the point (social agreement, internal dialogue, self-importance) and what moves it
- The positions the books name (the place of no pity, the second attention, the position of the dreaming body)
- The failure cases (sorcerers who got lost; the price of power plants; losing the human form)
- The books' own sign, if any, for a shift toward depth versus a shift toward chaos

**Target Audience:** our drift-direction work (New_6) and the writer; cite by book.

## Audio Guide Requests

### Audio 1: Debate — Leave the World or Revise It?

**NotebookLM Settings:**

- **Format:** Debate
- **Length:** Default

**Focus Prompt for NotebookLM:**
> Stage a debate between two readers of the books. One argues the warrior's path is an exit: the warrior "leaves the world," loses the human form, and the social world is only a description to be dropped. The other argues the path is a revision from inside: controlled folly, impeccability in ordinary tasks, the warrior who stays among people and acts with full commitment. Each side must cite books. End by stating what the books leave undecided.
