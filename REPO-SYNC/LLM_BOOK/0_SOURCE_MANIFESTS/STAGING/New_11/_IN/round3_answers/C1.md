## 1. The Demiurge vs. The Tonal
Castaneda's Equivalent: The Tonal (Tales of Power, Chapters 5 & 11; The Fire from Within, Chapter 7). Where the Map Holds: in Tales of Power (Chapter 5), the tonal is the social person, the organizer, judgment-maker, and assessor of the world. The tonal indeed believes it is the whole person: it creates words like "I" and "myself" to talk about itself, while actively suppressing or denying the existence of the nagual. Over time, social conditioning turns the tonal into a narrow-minded, "petty and despotic guard" obsessed with self-defense [17–18]. Where the Map BREAKS: the tonal is NOT "false" or evil. Unlike the Gnostic Demiurge (a false, corrupt ruler to be overthrown or destroyed), the books explicitly reject viewing the tonal as false or evil. In Tales of Power (Chapters 6 & 11), destroying or abandoning the tonal is fatal: "The tonal must be protected at any cost... If the tonal dies, so does the whole man". A warrior does not destroy the tonal; through practices like losing self-importance, the warrior "sweeps the island of the tonal clean" and "shrinks the tonal" so it can act as a healthy, broad-minded guardian alongside the nagual.

## 2. The Archons vs. Internal Dialogue, Habits, and "The Flyers"
Castaneda's Equivalents: Internal Dialogue, "Doing", and The Flyers' Mind. Where the Map Holds: in Journey to Ixtlan (Chapter 15) and Tales of Power (Chapter 1), habitual cognitive routines ("doing") and continuous "internal dialogue" function as automatic routines that maintain the agreed-upon description of reality below conscious choice [172–174]. [YouTube "Getting Castaneda": internal dialogue and repetitive habits as compulsive activities running below awareness to suppress the "other self".] Cross-Book Shift (The Archontic Paradigm Shift): early/middle books (Journey to Ixtlan / Tales of Power) describe internal dialogue and "doing" as human social conditioning taught to infants by adults [173–175]. Final book (The Active Side of Infinity, Chapter 16 "Mud Shadows"): our internal dialogue is literally a "foreign installation"—an alien mind given to humans by cosmic predators called "flyers" to consume our flares of awareness, keeping us trapped in self-reflection so we remain docile food [172–174, 185, 186]. This aligns directly with Archons.

## 3. The Divine Spark vs. The Nagual / The Double / Luminous Egg
Castaneda's Equivalents: The Nagual (Tales of Power, Chapter 5), The Energy Body / Double (Tales of Power; The Art of Dreaming, Chapter 1), and The Luminous Egg (The Fire from Within, Chapter 1). Where the Map Holds: in Tales of Power (Chapter 5) and The Fire from Within (Chapter 7), the nagual is the unnamable, eternal, limitless part of our total being that daily social habits suppress. [YouTube "Getting Castaneda": human existence possesses an unacknowledged "other self" (the double) that ordinary awareness ignores until the moment of death.] Where the Map BREAKS: no individual "self" in the nagual. The Gnostic "Divine Spark" implies an immortal, individual "true self" or core soul. In Tales of Power (Chapters 5 & 8), don Juan explicitly refutes this: in the nagual, there is no personal identity, no "I", no soul, and no individual ego [48–50]. The "self" exists exclusively as an item on top of the table of the tonal [19–20, 39]. In the nagual, a human being is revealed to be a temporary "colony, a conglomerate of separate and independent" awarenesses bound together briefly by the "glue of life" [48–50].

## 4. Gnosis vs. "Seeing" / "Stopping the World"
Castaneda's Equivalents: "Seeing" (A Separate Reality, Chapter 1; The Fire from Within, Chapter 7) and "Stopping the World" (Journey to Ixtlan, Intro; Tales of Power, Chapter 1). Where the Map Holds: "seeing" and "stopping the world" break the certainty that everyday reality is an absolute physical truth, revealing it to be merely an arbitrary, socialized "description" or "construction". Where the Map BREAKS: not intellectual recognition. In Tales of Power (Chapter 11) and The Fire from Within (Chapter 7), "seeing" is not an intellectual or philosophical realization ("talking about talking" on the island of the tonal) [108–110, 112]. "Seeing" is a physical, bodily/energetic act occurring when the assemblage point moves to directly perceive "energy as it flows in the universe" [108–110, 166].

In Tales of Power (Chapter 5) and The Fire from Within (Chapter 7), verbal naming ("talking to ourselves") is the continuous glue that locks the assemblage point in place and upholds the ordinary world description [172–174].
