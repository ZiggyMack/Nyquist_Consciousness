## 1. The Ally
Position 1 (Early): in The Teachings of Don Juan and A Separate Reality, an ally is a personified, bodily, or semi-anthropomorphic power contained in specific plants or nature; it possesses a "rule," whispers in the apprentice's ear as a "miniature man's voice," and can be physically handled or fought. Position 2 (Later): in Tales of Power, The Fire from Within, The Power of Silence, and The Art of Dreaming, allies are formless, non-human inorganic energy fields ("beings without corporeal essence"). What the Later Book Says: in The Fire from Within (Chapter 8) and The Power of Silence (Chapter 6), anthropomorphizing allies as speaking humanlike entities was a "prescribed means for deviating the apprentice's attention" away from internal dialogue so the energy body could shift.

## 2. The Power Plants
Position 1 (Early): in The Teachings of Don Juan and A Separate Reality, psychotropic plants are the central, indispensable vehicles for entering non-ordinary reality, meeting allies, and acquiring sorcery power [20–23]. Position 2 (Later): in Journey to Ixtlan, Tales of Power, The Fire from Within, and The Power of Silence, power plants are unnecessary, physically damaging "padding" or "pacifiers." What the Later Book Says: in Journey to Ixtlan (Introduction), Castaneda concedes his early focus on plants was an error caused by his own "lack of sensitivity." In Tales of Power (Chapter 11) and The Fire from Within (Chapter 7), don Juan confesses that he gave power plants only because Castaneda was so rigid and intellectual that he required violent physical shocks to stop his internal dialogue, despite the "untold damage" plants inflict on the body.

## 3. The Nature of the Double
Position 1 (Earlier/Middle): in Tales of Power (Chapters 1 & 2) and The Eagle's Gift (Chapter 12), the double / dreaming body is an exact, solid, three-dimensional physical replica of a sorcerer—capable of walking, eating, talking, and joking in two places at once. Position 2 (Later): in The Fire from Within (Chapter 8) and The Art of Dreaming (Chapter 1), the double / dreaming body is a non-human, impersonal "blob of light" or energy unit that travels along universal filaments, explicitly rejecting the physical human replica. What the Later Book Says: in The Fire from Within (Chapter 8), creating a dense physical human replica belonged to the ancient sorcerers' dangerous obsession with human form and self-reflection; "new seers" avoid copying the physical organism altogether.

## 4. What Happens at Death
Position 1 (Earlier): in Tales of Power (Chapter 5), death is the dissolution of the "glue of life," whereupon all individual units of awareness disintegrate, separate, and sink independently back into the nagual / unknown "as if there'd never been a unit". Position 2 (Later): in The Fire from Within (Chapters 3 & 15) and The Power of Silence (Introduction), death is the Eagle devouring the awareness of dying organic creatures, UNLESS a warrior achieves "total awareness" and kindles "the fire from within." What the Later Book Says: in The Fire from Within (Chapter 3) and The Art of Dreaming (Chapter 1), ordinary death involves the Eagle's attraction disintegrating awareness to consume it, but the new seers discovered the "third attention"—where all internal emanations light up simultaneously, allowing a warrior to retain life force, preserve awareness, and "dart past the Eagle" into infinity.

## 5. The Role of the Eagle
Position 1 (Earlier): in The Eagle's Gift (Chapter 9), the Eagle is an immeasurable, jet-black standing force reaching to infinity that bestows awareness at birth and devours the awareness of dying organic creatures like a swarm of fireflies [97, 363–364]. Position 2 (Later): in The Fire from Within (Chapter 3), don Juan criticizes and softens this, stating that calling this cosmic force an "Eagle" that "devours us" is a "grotesque" human projection created by "miserable" or "lax seers" [123–124]. What the Later Book Says: "Look at what some seers have done to us. We are stuck with their vision of an Eagle that rules us and devours us at the moment of our death... personally I don't appreciate the idea of something devouring us. For me, it would be more accurate to say that there is a force that attracts our consciousness, much as a magnet attracts iron shavings" [123–124].

## 6. The Account of the Teacher's Own Teacher
Position 1 (Early/Middle): in The Teachings of Don Juan, A Separate Reality, and Tales of Power, don Juan refers to his "benefactor" as a single primary figure. Position 2 (Later): in The Fire from Within (Chapter 12) and The Power of Silence (Chapters 1 & 6), don Juan reveals he actually had two opposite teachers: his primary benefactor (a flamboyant actor and master stalker) AND that benefactor's own benefactor (a quiet, conservative dreamer) [154, 292, 302–304]. What the Later Book Says: in The Power of Silence (Chapter 1), his case was unique: he had "eight years' grace" overlapping with the elder nagual, being reared by both a "powerful father" and "even more powerful grandfather," and is properly the fine-tuned product of the elder's teachings.

## 7. Status of the Tonal/Nagual Model After the Assemblage Point Model Arrives
Position 1 (Middle): in Tales of Power (Part 2), reality and human consciousness are explained through the dualism of the tonal (the social person, reason, the known, the island) and the nagual (the unnamable, the unknown, power, creativity) [5, 33–34]. Position 2 (Late): in The Fire from Within (Chapters 3 & 7) and The Art of Dreaming (Chapter 1), perception is explained through the geometric model of the assemblage point, luminous cocoon, and Eagle's emanations (first, second, and third attention) [10–12, 126–127, 218–223]. What the Later Book Says: in The Fire from Within (Chapter 7) and The Power of Silence (Chapter 1), the earlier terms are mapped onto the new geometric model: the tonal is the emphasized emanations of the right side / first attention, the nagual is the unaligned emanations of the left side / second attention, and the assemblage point is the actual physical mechanism on the luminous egg that dictates which emanations are illuminated.

## 8. Whether the Apprentice's Cohort Existed During the Early Books
Position 1 (Early): in The Teachings of Don Juan, A Separate Reality, Journey to Ixtlan, and Tales of Power, Castaneda is an isolated student trained alone by the teacher and the benefactor. Position 2 (Later): in The Second Ring of Power (Chapters 1 & 3), The Eagle's Gift (Chapter 1) [and the YouTube audiobook], don Juan had a full, structured cohort of nine other apprentices AND a second compact group of three [real persons; excluded from story use] [41–44, 82, 214]. What the Later Book Says: in The Second Ring of Power (Chapter 1) and The Eagle's Gift (Chapters 1 & 14), don Juan deliberately forbade him from knowing about or writing about the other apprentices during his early training because they were trained in heightened awareness or belonged to different energetic fields ("don Juan did not permit me to do so... the agreement I had with him was to write about his field, not mine").

Offered follow-ups: the "recapitulation breath"; the "art of stalking".
