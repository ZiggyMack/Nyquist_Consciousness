## 1. Catalog of Mirrors, Reflections, and Reflecting Surfaces

A. The Submerged Mirror in a Stream (The Toltec Mirror Technique). The Fire from Within (Chapter 4 "Inorganic Beings") [136–143, 494–501]. Who: Castaneda and don Juan (an ancient Toltec technique passed down from the Nagual Julian and the Nagual Elias). What it shows: when submerged in shallow running water, the mirror disarranges ordinary reflection, magnifies focus (showing forehead skin pores in extreme detail), and reveals a 3D picture of the "depths" (the first level of the fluidity of water). It also reveals a non-human, headlike shape resting on the edge of the glass [137–138, 495–496]. What it lets through: the mirror acts as a "window", "bait", or "hatch" through which an inorganic being (an ally from the first level of water's fluidity) can cross the boundary into the physical world. What it traps: the practitioner's gaze and attention, through an intense, magnetic suction force that pulls the practitioner toward the depths or locks them in an inescapable grip. What it costs: mortally dangerous; without a benefactor to break the spell, the water and ally will drown or trap the practitioner permanently. When the Nagual Julian performed the technique, his wooden frame broke, allowing the ally to climb out and chase him around a hill until his benefactor built a fire to burn off his fear. Looking into the mirror produces a deep, unexplainable sadness and fear of "the absolute loneliness of eternity" and the "puniness of humanity".

B. Water Gazed Into / Reflecting Water Surfaces. A Separate Reality (Chapters 11 & 12) & The Fire from Within (Chapter 4) [39, 41–42, 137]. Who: Castaneda (guided by don Juan) and ancient water gazers. What it shows: fixes moving water into an immobile, frozen "picture of water" with razor-sharp ripples, which then expands into a dazzling green fog or tiny basketball-sized bubbles. What it lets through / where it transports: acts as a "hinge" or vehicle for bodily or perceptual transportation across vast distances [41–42, 46]. What it traps: the gazer's mind in the "clutches of the green fog" [41–42]. What it costs: risk of drowning or permanent disorientation if the practitioner succumbs to the water without an impeccable guide to pull them out [41–42, 50].

C. Car Rearview Mirrors. A Separate Reality (Chapter 2). Who: Castaneda while driving with don Juan. What it shows: car headlights appearing and disappearing over hilltops in the dark, closing in on them. What it traps / costs: Castaneda's attention in suspense, paranoia, and obsessive rational tracking of an oncoming vehicle.

D. The Eyes as Mirrors and Reflectors. Tales of Power (Chapters 2, 7, 8, 11) & The Power of Silence (Chapters 1, 6, 9, 14) [70, 78, 174–175, 184, 324–325, 349]. Reflecting Light: Castaneda reflects eastern sunlight on his cornea to soothe his body after intense physical shock. The Nagual as an Unreflecting Mirror: don Juan tells Castaneda: "You are a mirror that doesn't reflect our images. You are already beyond our reach". Catchers / Reflectors of Intent: the eyes act as "catchers of intent". A seer uses the "shine of their eyes" (the shimmering film of the "place of no pity") to move their own or an apprentice's assemblage point [124, 174–175, 324–325]. What it costs: absolute ruthlessness (the loss of self-pity); using the gaze for self-importance or mesmerizing others drains energy and destroys sobriety.

E. "Figures in Front of a Mirror". The Active Side of Infinity (Chapter 2 "The Intent of Infinity"). Who: an aging dancer performs for Castaneda and a friend, twirling and performing "figures in front of a mirror" to a Victrola march [207, 553–554]. What it illustrates / costs: don Juan uses this as a stark existential metaphor: "every one of us, young and old alike, is making figures in front of a mirror in one way or another... the result of their actions is always the same: senseless figures in front of a mirror". It costs a human lifetime wasted on empty self-reflection.

F. The Double as a Reflection of the Self. Tales of Power (Chapters 1, 2, 8, 11), The Eagle's Gift (Chapter 9), and the YouTube source "Getting Castaneda" [66, 69, 72, 113, 388–391]. The Other as the Self: "the other is the self" / "the double is the self"—a complete, bright, corporeal manifestation of the sorcerer's energy body developed through dreaming. Female Warrior Mirroring: in The Eagle's Gift, twin female warriors of each direction "are so alike that they mirror each other, and only through impeccability can they find solace and challenge in each other's reflection". [YouTube source, commentary]: the double is described as the "other self" born from our two-step process of perception, living in direct perception while the daily self lives in secondary recollections. What it costs: developing the double requires total erasure of personal history, self-importance, and routines. Facing one's double face-to-face is fatal: "the sorcerer that finds himself face to face with himself is a dead sorcerer".

## 2. How the Books Use "Reflection" as a Word
Optical/Physical Reflection: light or images bouncing off surfaces (submerged mirrors, water, eyelids, or eyes) [70, 136–138]. Mental/Energetic Self-Reflection: the cognitive process where human awareness turns inward to take an inventory of its own contents, constructing a self-image and generating self-importance [132–133, 176–177]. In Tales of Power (Chapter 11), human beings live inside a sealed "bubble of perception" where "what we witness on its round walls is our own reflection"—our own description of the world.

## 3. Is "Self-Reflection" the Enemy, the Instrument, or Both?
A. Primarily the Enemy / The Trap. In The Fire from Within (Chapters 2, 7, 8) and The Power of Silence (Chapters 1, 6, 12), self-reflection is the primary enemy of warriorship [132, 176–177, 330–334]: it generates self-importance (self-pity in disguise), locking the assemblage point rigidly into its habitual position; "it was self-reflection that had disconnected man from the spirit in the first place"; the main duty of a Nagual/teacher is to "shatter the mirror of self-reflection" in the apprentice to free energy.
B. Instrumental / Natural Origin (With Limits). In The Fire from Within (Chapter 7), taking an inventory and possessing a basic degree of self-reflection is an obligatory command from the Eagle for organic beings to interact with their environment. But human beings carry this command to its extreme—turning inventory into self-absorption—so it becomes a destructive trap [132–133]. Basic inventory is a natural function; indulging in self-reflection is the enemy that must be shattered to allow the assemblage point to move.

## 4. Cross-Book Shifts
Early (The Teachings of Don Juan / A Separate Reality): water and reflecting surfaces are mystical doors or shamanic tools used to perceive non-ordinary reality or navigate with plant allies [39, 41–42]. Later (The Fire from Within): don Juan reinterprets these through energetic geometry: the submerged mirror is an ancient Toltec technique that manipulates water's fluidity to pull inorganic beings through a "window". He criticizes the ancient seers for getting trapped by their obsession with these reflecting practices, warning that they lead to morbidity, fixation, and loss of freedom [138–143, 496–501].
