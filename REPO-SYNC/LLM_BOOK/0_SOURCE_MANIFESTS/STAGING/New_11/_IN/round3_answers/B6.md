## 1. What the Teacher, Nagual, and Benefactor Can and Cannot Do

A. The Teacher "Only Points" / The Apprentice Must Do the Work. Tales of Power (Chapters 1 & 8), The Fire from Within (Chapter 7), The Power of Silence (Chapter 6), and The Art of Dreaming (Chapter 1). What the Teacher Cannot Do: a teacher cannot give an apprentice personal power, will, or enlightenment as a "free handout". In The Power of Silence (Chapter 6), "there is very little value in instruction" because instruction alone cannot move the assemblage point. In The Art of Dreaming (Chapter 1), "every sorcerer has to prove everything with his own experience". What the Apprentice Must Do: in Tales of Power (Chapter 1), an apprentice cannot rely on a mentor's perception: "You are the one who's learning, therefore you yourself must claim knowledge as power... You yourself have to be convinced that you can exercise your will". The apprentice must store their own personal power, execute their own tasks, and face the unknown on the strength of their own impeccability.

B. The Teacher Uses Tricks, Deception, and "The Nagual's Blow". A Separate Reality (Chapter 14), Tales of Power (Chapters 1 & 8), The Fire from Within (Chapter 7), and The Power of Silence (Chapters 1 & 6). Tricks and Deception: in A Separate Reality (Chapter 14) and Tales of Power (Chapter 8), don Juan bluntly states: "The rule is that a man of knowledge has to trap his apprentice... Today I have trapped you and I have tricked you into learning". Because human reason rigidly defends its self-reflection, a teacher must use "positive trickery," context disarrangement, and decoy tasks (such as pretending "seeing" is a learnable skill or assigning silly chores) to distract reason while trapping the apprentice's attention. The Nagual's Blow: in The Fire from Within (Chapter 7) and The Power of Silence (Introduction), the Nagual uses extra energy to deliver "the nagual's blow"—a strike or push delivered to a precise spot on the luminous egg over the right shoulder blade. This creates a temporary dent in the cocoon, displacing the glow of awareness into neighboring emanations and thrusting the apprentice into heightened awareness.

C. How the Benefactor's Role Differs from the Teacher's. Tales of Power (Chapters 7 & 8) [41–42, 58–59]. The Teacher (works inside the bubble): the teacher's duty is strictly with the tonal—to clean, sweep, and reorder "the island of the tonal" on the right half of the bubble of perception, forcing the apprentice into reasonableness, sobriety, and physical strength. The Benefactor (works outside the bubble): the benefactor addresses the left side—the nagual. Once the teacher has swept half of the bubble clean, the benefactor opens the bubble from the outside, delivering the neophyte directly to power, guiding them into the unmapped dimensions of the nagual, and splitting their luminosity [41–42, 46, 58–59]. Don Juan in Tales of Power (Chapter 8): "In these matters one can either talk or act... One either takes the island of the tonal or one takes the nagual. In your case my duty has been to work with your tonal".

## 2. The Farewell
A. How don Juan and don Genaro Leave. Tales of Power (Chapter 14 & Epilogue), The Eagle's Gift (Chapter 15), The Fire from Within (Chapter 18 & Epilogue), and The Active Side of Infinity (Chapter 17) [68–69, 92, 113, 151, 655, 757]. On a flat, rocky mountaintop mesa in southern Mexico, don Juan, don Genaro, and the fifteen seers of their party complete their cycle. Kindling all the emanations of awareness inside their luminous cocoons at once, they are consumed by "a fire from within". They convert their physical organisms into pure energy, transforming into a row of "exquisite lights in the sky" resembling a plumed serpent. They float and circle above the peak in a final survey of the earth before vanishing into the "third attention" and eternity.
B. What the Apprentice is Left With. Tales of Power (Chapter 14 & Epilogue), The Second Ring of Power (Chapters 1 & 3), The Fire from Within (Epilogue), and The Active Side of Infinity (Chapter 17) [64–67, 69, 113, 150–151, 272]. The Jump into the Abyss: the apprentices (Castaneda, Pablito, and Nestor) are left alone at the edge of the precipice. By the force of their personal power alone, they leap off the cliff into the six-hundred-foot gorge. They do not smash on the rocks; the shock moves their assemblage points, shattering their normal perception and assembling other worlds. Solitude and the Unfulfilled Task: Castaneda returns to the ordinary world completely alone, stripped of his teacher and benefactor. He is left with no ongoing guidance, no material shields, and no one to pull him back from the unknown [64, 150–151, 271]. He retains only his personal power, his impeccability, and his assigned task—a special life situation given by his mentors that he must wait like a warrior to fulfill before he can claim command over the totality of himself [64, 66, 150–151].

## 3. What the "Blueprint" Is and What is Explicitly Withheld
A. The "Blueprint" in the Books' Own Terms. The Abstract Cores: in The Power of Silence (Chapter 1), don Juan explicitly defines the abstract cores as the "blueprint for events, or a recurrent pattern that appeared every time intent was giving an indication of something meaningful". There are 21 abstract cores in sorcery that form a structural template through which intent operates for every generation of apprentices [763, 1052–1053]. The Rule (The Map): in The Teachings of Don Juan (Unit 3) and The Eagle's Gift (Chapter 9 "The Rule of the Nagual"), the teacher transmits "The Rule"—an inflexible, non-cumulative organizing framework dictated by the Eagle [5, 81–84, 332, 608]. The Rule acts as a pragmatic map laying out the structural composition of a warrior's party (sixteen warriors: stalkers, dreamers, couriers, and double beings) and the sequential stages of a warrior's development [82–84, 608].
B. What is Explicitly Withheld. Direct Experience and Realization Cannot Be Handed Down: in The Eagle's Gift (Chapter 9) and The Power of Silence (Chapter 6), don Juan's own benefactor never gave direct knowledge as a handout, believing that "knowledge imparted that way lacked effectiveness". The "How" of the Nagual and Intent: in Tales of Power (Chapters 7 & 8) and The Fire from Within (Chapter 7), while a sorcerer knows how to reach the nagual, how the nagual or intent operates once activated is unexplainable and unknowable; words stop there. The Outcome or Guarantee of Success: in Tales of Power (Chapter 14) and The Eagle's Gift (Chapter 9), no teacher can guarantee or withhold an apprentice's ultimate fate; the outcome of entering the unknown is governed entirely by impersonal power, intent, and the apprentice's own impeccability.

## 4. Shifts and Disagreements Across the Books
Plant-Induced Consensus vs. Energetic Assemblage Point Moves: early (The Teachings of Don Juan) the teacher's role is framed as providing "special consensus" to validate state changes induced by hallucinogenic plant allies; later (Tales of Power / The Fire from Within) power plants are dismissed as crude, dangerous shock tactics and the teacher's true role is reordered as cleaning the tonal and delivering "the nagual's blow" to move the assemblage point directly through inner silence.
The Rule as a Physical Passageway vs. Abstract Map: in The Eagle's Gift (Chapter 9), don Juan confesses that early in his training he got stuck treating the Rule as a literal physical opening or door in the world. He later realized the Rule is an abstract energetic map, and his teaching method was designed to prevent Castaneda from repeating his mistake.
