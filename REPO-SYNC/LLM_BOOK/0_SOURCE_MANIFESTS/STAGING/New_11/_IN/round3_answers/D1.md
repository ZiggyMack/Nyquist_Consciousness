## 1. What the Books and Sources Claim About Their Truth Status
Academic Ethnography & Field Notes: The Teachings of Don Juan is presented initially as anthropological fieldwork, structured into selections from his "field notes" on non-ordinary reality, followed by an academic "Structural Analysis" attempting to classify don Juan's conceptual units. A Separate Reality is explicitly defined as a "reportage" employing the "phenomenological method", where Castaneda attempts to record non-ordinary experiences while suspending Western judgment.
Reframing as "Stopping the World": Journey to Ixtlan re-evaluates his ten years of research. Castaneda discards his original plant-centered ethnography framework, claiming his early focus on hallucinogens was an error caused by his own "lack of sensitivity," and reframes the apprenticeship around behavioral "techniques for stopping the world".
Transition to Autobiography: The Eagle's Gift explicitly declares that the work has abandoned traditional anthropology: "Although I am an anthropologist, this is not strictly an anthropological work... my work has been transformed into an autobiography... I must first of all reiterate that this is not a work of fiction."
Narrative of Awareness & Energy Recovery: The Fire from Within is described as a "narrative of what happened, as it happened," detailing hidden "teachings for the left side" (heightened awareness) that took a decade to recall. The Power of Silence is framed as a "true account of a teaching method" designed to illustrate the abstract cores of intent. The Active Side of Infinity is formatted not as field notes, but as a shamanic "collection of memorable events"—a practical device recommended by don Juan to "stir caches of energy" and prepare for the "definitive journey".

## 2. Where Castaneda Concedes the Account is Shaped, Reconstructed, or Rearranged
Chronological & Dialogue Editing: in the Introduction to The Teachings of Don Juan, his notes "are not always in proper chronological sequence" and he "condensed long pages of questions and answers... to avoid reproducing the repetitiveness of conversation". In the Introduction to A Separate Reality: "In order to present the intricacies of this learning process in a readable form I have condensed long passages of questions and answers, and thus I have edited my original field notes".
Subjective Classification & Translation Bias: in "A Structural Analysis" (The Teachings of Don Juan), don Juan's concepts "could not be the exact duplicate of what he said himself" because their meaning was "deflected by my own attempts to classify them".
Selective Omission & Re-indexing: in Journey to Ixtlan, he concedes that he initially suppressed and discarded the vast majority of don Juan's non-plant teachings during his first decade of writing because they did not fit his academic thesis on hallucinogens.
Linear Reconstruction of Memory Loss (The 15-Year Gap): in The Power of Silence and The Art of Dreaming, because don Juan conducted his most vital lessons in "heightened awareness," Castaneda suffered total amnesia upon returning to normal consciousness. In the Author's Note to The Art of Dreaming, it took "15 years of uninterrupted work from 1973 to 1988 to store enough energy to rearrange everything linearly in my mind", filling in memory lapses to create a coherent narrative continuity.
