## 1. The Warrior's Witness (The Detached Observer)
The warrior's witness is not a passive mental spectator, but a state of heightened, unemotional awareness cultivated through rigorous discipline. Cultivation via Controlled Folly: in A Separate Reality (Chapter 5) and Tales of Power (Chapter 11), the warrior cultivates a detached stance called controlled folly—acting with total commitment while knowing that "nothing is more important than anything else", participating fully without becoming emotionally tangled or attached to outcomes. Cultivation via "Seeing" and Shedding Self-Pity: in A Separate Reality (Chapter 1) and The Fire from Within (Chapter 7), direct perception requires shutting off the internal dialogue and shedding the "human form" (self-pity and self-importance). An impeccable warrior observes horrific or extraordinary events with absolute sobriety: "Impeccable warriors don't lose their marbles... [they] remain untouched". Reference File Mapping: the digest labels this state the "Warrior's witness", mapping it to a non-dual observer architecture. [Our label, not the books'.]

## 2. "The Double"
Tales of Power (Chapters 1, 2, 8, & 11), The Second Ring of Power (Chapter 3), The Active Side of Infinity (Chapter 17), and the YouTube commentary "Getting Castaneda". In Tales of Power (Chapter 2): "The double is the self". It is an act of power developed through dreaming, where a sorcerer's energy body becomes an exact, corporeal manifestation of the practitioner in the physical world. In The Active Side of Infinity (Chapter 17), the double is the energy body brought into an exact, three-dimensional, solid replica of the physical organism through discipline. [YouTube commentary: the double as the "other self" born from the two-step process of human perception; the daily self lives in secondary recollections, the double in direct perception.]

## 3. "The Dreaming Body"
The Eagle's Gift (Chapters 12 & 14), The Fire from Within (Chapter 8), and The Art of Dreaming (Chapter 1). In The Eagle's Gift (Chapter 12) and The Art of Dreaming (Chapter 1), the "dreaming body" is the "not-doing of sleep"—a phantomlike energy unit projected when the second attention fixates on the total energy field of the luminous being. Cross-Book Evolution & Disagreement: earlier/middle books (Tales of Power / The Eagle's Gift) use dreaming body and double almost interchangeably for an exact physical replica of a sorcerer (a double appearing solid, walking, and joking). Later (The Fire from Within, Chapter 8 & The Art of Dreaming, Chapter 1), don Juan draws a sharp distinction and retracts the physical replica goal: creating a dense, physical human replica belonged to the ancient sorcerers' dangerous obsession with human form. The "new seers" do not copy the physical body at all; for them, the dreaming body is strictly an impersonal "blob of light" or a surge of energy that travels along universal filaments.

## 4. Which Concept Matches the "Watcher," and What the Books Add
Which Matches Best: The Warrior's Witness / Detached Observer is the closest match. Like the Watcher, it "does not drift when the rest of the person does" (maintaining unbending intent and sobriety during emotional chaos) and is "enacted" through physical practices (controlled folly, inner silence, and stalking) rather than measured theoretically. The Double and The Dreaming Body DO NOT match the Watcher: they are not passive internal observers; they are active, energetic vehicles that execute physical acts, travel through space, and assemble alternate worlds.
What the Books Add That the Definition Lacks:
- Physicality and Energetic Mass: the double / dreaming body is not a psychological or mental observer, but a condensed, three-dimensional emanation of pure luminosity capable of exerting physical force, going through walls, and traveling across layers of the universe (Tales of Power, Chapter 2; The Fire from Within, Chapter 8).
- The "Who Dreams Whom" Inversion: in Tales of Power (Chapter 8), The Second Ring of Power (Chapter 3) [and the YouTube commentary], it is actually the double that dreams the ordinary daily self, reversing the assumption that the waking ego is the primary owner of awareness.
- Assemblage Point Mechanics: both witness-observation and double-creation are governed by the precise spatial movement and fixation of the assemblage point on the surface or interior of the luminous sphere (The Fire from Within, Chapter 7; The Art of Dreaming, Chapter 1).
- Lethal Incompatibility of Simultaneous Self-Facing: while an internal Watcher is assumed to observe the mind safely, confronting one's double face-to-face in ordinary awareness is fatal: "the sorcerer that finds himself face to face with himself is a dead sorcerer" (Tales of Power, Chapter 2).

Offered follow-ups: the "seven gates of dreaming" used to forge the dreaming body; "recapitulation".
