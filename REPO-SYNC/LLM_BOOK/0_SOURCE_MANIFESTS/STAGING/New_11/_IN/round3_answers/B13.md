## 1. What the Teacher Deliberately Withholds and Why
The teacher deliberately withholds the true nature of sorcery operations, the true purpose of assigned tasks, and the underlying mechanics of perceptual shifts—often for years or for the entire duration of initial training. The Core Reason: in The Fire from Within (Chapter 7) and The Power of Silence (Chapter 6), direct, verbal explanations are withheld because "knowledge imparted that way lacked effectiveness. It was never there when one needed it". Because human reason (the tonal) compulsively seeks to rationalize, explain, and maintain control, telling an apprentice the truth upfront causes their rational mind to analyze, judge, or reject the instruction. The teacher must withhold the true explanation to distract the "attention of the tonal" with pseudo-tasks so that the energy body can act and the assemblage point can shift directly [71–73, 116, 281].

## 2. Catalog of Delayed Revelations
A. The Plants as "Padding" / Decoy Tools. Withheld in: The Teachings of Don Juan and A Separate Reality. Revealed in: Tales of Power (Chapter 11) & The Fire from Within (Chapter 7). Power plants were merely "padding," "pacifiers," or crude shock tactics, used only because Castaneda was an exceptionally "sassy," rigid, and intellectual student who needed violent physical jolts to stop his internal dialogue and move his assemblage point, despite the "untold damage" plants inflict on the body.
B. Staged Encounters & The "Worthy Opponent". Withheld in: A Separate Reality (Chapters 1 & 14). Revealed in: Tales of Power (Chapter 11), Journey to Ixtlan (Chapter 15), and The Power of Silence (Chapters 1 & 7). The sorceress was never a threat to don Juan's life; she was a "worthy opponent" brought in as a deliberate trap. Withholding was necessary because, after plant-induced shocks, an apprentice naturally wants to quit; the staged threat forced Castaneda to choose between his soft ordinary life and the warrior's path. Similarly, staged encounters at the bus station or in Guaymas were tricks of "masked ruthlessness" designed to shatter psychological continuity and force the assemblage point into the place of no pity [101–102, 107–108, 325].
C. The Benefactor's Deceptions (of don Juan in his youth). Withheld in: don Juan's early apprenticeship (he believed he was held captive by a monstrous fish-faced man and saved by a frail old couple). Revealed in: The Fire from Within (Chapters 2 & 12) and The Power of Silence (Chapters 1 & 6) [115–119, 129, 281–284]. There was no physical monster—it was an inorganic energy field. The benefactor had disguised himself as a frail old man and created the monster drama to cushion the terrifying impact on don Juan's body, trap his full participation, and force his assemblage point to shift into heightened awareness [116–117, 129, 281].
D. The "Sorcerers' Explanation". Withheld in: The Teachings of Don Juan, A Separate Reality, and Journey to Ixtlan (across 13 years of apprenticeship). Revealed in: Tales of Power (Part 3 "The Sorcerers' Explanation"). The foundational explanation of the tonal, the nagual, the bubble of perception, and the assemblage point was held back until the very end. Revealing it earlier would have been useless "pep talk" or rational chatter; an apprentice must first accumulate personal power and clean their island of the tonal before the explanation can deliver its intended energetic impact.
E. The Trick of the Apprenticeship Itself & Pseudo-Tasks. Withheld in: The Teachings of Don Juan, A Separate Reality, and Journey to Ixtlan. Revealed in: Tales of Power (Chapter 11) and The Power of Silence (Chapter 6). Tasks like "learning to see" or performing senseless chores were "pseudo-tasks". Because human beings are "nincompoops" who refuse to relinquish rational control voluntarily, the teacher must trick the apprentice's attention with decoy tasks to teach the real lesson: stopping internal dialogue and "acting without expecting rewards".

## 3. What the Apprentice is NEVER Told, and Why
- Their Specific Warrior Task / Ultimate Assignment. The Eagle's Gift (Chapter 14). The specific, long-term warrior task assigned to an apprentice by intent is kept secret by fellow warriors and mentors. Reason: the apprentice must prove their own impeccability, store their own energy, and claim knowledge through personal power rather than relying on verbal directions.
- Direct Verbal Descriptions of Intent and the Unknowable. The Fire from Within (Chapter 7) & The Power of Silence (Chapter 6) [125, 270–271, 320, 347, 498, 501]. A teacher never gives a direct, literal verbal definition or formula for intent, silent knowledge, or the unknowable. Reason: intent pertains to the realm of energy and direct perception, not reason or language. Words reduce the abstract to "states of intuition" or empty intellectualism; true knowledge of intent can only be experienced directly through silent knowledge when the assemblage point moves.
- The Existence of Parallel Apprentice Groups. The Second Ring of Power (Chapter 1) & The Art of Dreaming (Chapter 1). Don Juan never informed Castaneda about his second group of apprentices during his active apprenticeship. Reason: the second group belonged exclusively to Castaneda's energetic field, and the agreement between them was for Castaneda to write about don Juan's field, not his own.

## 4. Shifts Across the Decades
Early (The Teachings of Don Juan): the apprenticeship is framed as a literal, step-by-step transmission of plant knowledge, rules, and consensus. Later (The Power of Silence): "There is very little value in instruction... I've taught you all kinds of things in order to trap your attention... Moving the assemblage point is all that matters".
