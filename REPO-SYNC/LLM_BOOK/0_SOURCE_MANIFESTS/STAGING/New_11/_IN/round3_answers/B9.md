## 1. Definition: Stalking vs. Dreaming
The Eagle's Gift (Chapter 14), The Fire from Within (Chapters 11 & 12), and The Art of Dreaming (Chapter 1). Dreaming: the art of displacing or unhooking the assemblage point from its habitual location during sleep or heightened awareness. Stalking: the art of fixating the assemblage point on any new position to which it has been displaced, thereby acquiring cohesion and stability in that new state [184–185, 199]. In daily human terms, stalking is the deliberate, surreptitious manipulation of one's own behavior—using novel, deceptive, or non-routine actions with people to unhook perception from self-reflection without taking oneself seriously [106, 126, 137, 231–232].

## 2. The Seven Principles of Stalking
The Eagle's Gift (Chapter 14 "The Principles of Stalking") [80–81, 85].
1. Choose the battleground—never go into any battle without knowing the exact surroundings.
2. Discard the unnecessary—strip away every superfluous detail and habit.
3. Apply total concentration to decide—be fully ready and willing to make one's last stand here and now, without hesitation [81, 295–296].
4. Relax and abandon oneself—fear nothing, allowing the guiding powers of intent to open the path.
5. Retreat when facing impossible odds—when overwhelmed, step back temporarily and occupy the mind with something else.
6. Compress time—do not waste a single second, treating an instant as an eternity in a battle for survival.
7. Never push to the front—always operate from behind the scenes, using a decoy or front.
The Four Moods of Stalking: in The Fire from Within (Chapter 12) and The Power of Silence (Chapter 6): ruthlessness (without harshness), cunning (without cruelty), patience (without negligence), and sweetness (without foolishness).

## 3. The First Stalking Exercise Given to an Apprentice
The Eagle's Gift (Chapter 14) & The Power of Silence (Chapter 6). Scene-able Action: Stalking Oneself via Recapitulation: the apprentice sits inside a wooden crate or enclosed space, writes out a list of every person and interaction in their life, and systematically relives each memory. The Stalker's Breath: starting with the chin on the right shoulder, the apprentice inhales slowly while turning the head 180 degrees to the left shoulder to retrieve energy filaments left behind in the interaction; then exhales turning from left to right to eject foreign energy filaments. Self-Observation: the apprentice "stalks himself" by intentionally catching and disrupting their own behavioral obsessions, self-pity, and routines [126, 127, 231–232].

## 4. How the Teacher Uses a Petty Tyrant as a Lesson Plan
The Fire from Within (Chapter 2 "Petty Tyrants") [92, 94–96]. Scene-able Action: the teacher intentionally places or encourages the apprentice in a high-stress workplace or social situation under an abusive, unreasonable tormentor [92–94]. Rather than fighting back or indulging in anger/pity, the apprentice uses four specific attributes as a physical game: Control (fulfilling the tyrant's most unreasonable, humiliating commands cheerfully and impeccably without letting pride get bruised); Discipline (calmly gathering data on the tyrant's habits, weaknesses, and fears while actively being harassed [94–95]); Forbearance (waiting patiently with joy and no hatred, holding back counter-action [95–96]); Timing (executing a sudden, unexpected counter-move that taunts or neutralizes the tyrant, while taking cover behind a higher social/legal shield such as an employer's boss or a religious authority [95–96]).

## 5. "Death as an Advisor" Taught as a Physical Practice
A Separate Reality (Chapter 12) & Journey to Ixtlan (Chapter 4) [46, 127, 232–233, 281]. When Practiced: whenever the apprentice feels impatient, offended, annoyed, or overwhelmed by difficulties. The Physical Action: the teacher commands the apprentice to jerk or turn their head suddenly to the left and look over their left shoulder at an arm's length, where death is constantly standing as an eternal companion. The Sensory Response: the physical gesture triggers an involuntary stomach contraction and a physical "chill" down the spine. Recognizing that death has not yet tapped them, the apprentice instantly drops all self-importance and pettiness, performing the next immediate task as if it were their last battle on earth.

## 6. "Erasing Personal History" Assigned as a Task
Journey to Ixtlan (Chapter 2) & Tales of Power (Chapter 1) [41–43, 61, 280, 286]. Scene-able Action: the teacher refuses to let the apprentice fill out personal history charts, family trees, or state their background. The apprentice is ordered to stop giving explanations to family, friends, and acquaintances regarding what they are doing or where they are going [42–43]. The apprentice systematically "chopped off" personal history bit by bit—telling stories that do not reveal their real self, deliberately keeping their daily movements unpredictable, and creating a "fog" so that no one knows for sure who they are or what they do [42–43, 280, 286].

## 7. Cross-Book Shifts and Reference Note
In early books (Journey to Ixtlan), erasing personal history and death as an advisor are presented as behavioral hunter/warrior strategies to break habits [42–43, 46, 280, 286]. In middle and late books (The Eagle's Gift / The Fire from Within), these practices are explicitly integrated under the art of stalking and energetic geometry: erasing personal history and recapitulation serve to strip away foreign energy filaments and unhook the assemblage point from self-reflection [83, 85, 106, 126, 231–232].
