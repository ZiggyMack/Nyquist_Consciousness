Here is the analysis of the absolute thresholds presented across Carlos Castaneda's books, detailing what crosses each threshold, its reversibility, and how the books' explanations shifted over time.

1. The "Crack Between the Worlds" / The "Edge" of the Known
Book Titles & Chapters: The Teachings of Don Juan (Chapter 11); Tales of Power (Chapter 14); The Second Ring of Power (Chapter 3).
What Crosses It: The physical body, individual awareness, or the sorcerer's double.
Mechanism & Threshold: In early accounts, the crack appears after prolonged body convulsions and shaking as a "monumental door, a crack that goes up and down" through which a practitioner must slide into another realm. In later accounts, la Gorda explains that the crack is the actual capacity to shift levels of attention between the daily world and non-ordinary reality.
Reversibility: Both Reversible and Irreversible. During training, a sorcerer like don Genaro can go "in and out of the crack between the worlds" like walking through a door. However, when don Juan and don Genaro jumped off the cliff at the climax of Tales of Power, they went through the crack permanently and "didn't come back".
Cross-Book Disagreement/Shift: In The Teachings of Don Juan, entering the crack is tied to psychotropic plant convulsions and framed as entering a literal physical/spiritual gap. By The Second Ring of Power and The Fire from Within, don Juan and la Gorda reframe the crack as a metaphor for disengaging the "attention of the tonal" and shifting the assemblage point.

2. A Position of the Assemblage Point Beyond Which There Is No Return
Book Titles & Chapters: The Fire from Within (Chapters 7 & 8); The Art of Dreaming (Chapters 1 & 2) [168, 170–171, 248–249, 251, 254].
What Crosses It: The assemblage point (and consequently the entire luminous cocoon and awareness) [106, 170–171, 251].
Mechanism & Threshold: When the assemblage point moves outside the boundaries of the luminous cocoon or creates a permanent "crevice" in the energy egg rather than a temporary depression, the ordinary world vanishes completely [102, 112, 168, 248–249]. If the assemblage point moves into non-human emanations without sufficient discipline, energy, or impeccability, the practitioner cannot return. Ancient sorcerers pushed their assemblage points into lines of energy a thousand times their original size, transforming themselves into "lines of energy" rather than luminous spheres [170–171, 251, 254].
Reversibility: Irreversible. Once an ancient sorcerer's energy shape solidified into a line, "line cohesion set in" and made it impossible to revert to an egg-like shape. Similarly, if an average person's assemblage point shifts drastically due to madness or death, it cannot assemble the everyday world again.
Cross-Book Disagreement/Shift: In Tales of Power, entering the unknown was described as a dissolution into separate awarenesses bound only by intent. In The Fire from Within and The Art of Dreaming, this is formalized geometrically as moving the assemblage point along or outside the disk of emanations. Ancient sorcerers are criticized by the "new seers" for getting permanently stuck as disfigured lines of energy due to greed for power [171, 253–254].

3. "The Place of No Pity"
Book Titles & Chapters: The Fire from Within (Chapter 7) [100–101]; The Power of Silence (Chapters 4, 8, 9, & 10) [149–150, 153, 164, 345, 350, 390].
What Crosses It: The assemblage point shifts along the band of human emanations to a specific location where self-reflection and self-compassion collapse.
Mechanism & Threshold: Reached when a warrior curtails self-importance ("self-pity in disguise") and enters inner silence. The shift manifests physically as a "shimmering film" or bright shine over the eyes. At this position, ordinary emotional attachments dissolve, replacing sentimental sympathy with cold, impeccable ruthlessness.
Reversibility: Reversible / Fluctuating Initially. In The Power of Silence, don Juan emphasizes that when an apprentice first reaches the place of no pity, the shift is "extremely transitory" and the assemblage point "unavoidably returns to its point of departure". Only through long-term discipline and recapitulation does a warrior make this position stable, creating a new, permanent form of continuity.

4. The Third Attention
Book Titles & Chapters: The Eagle's Gift (Chapters 1 & 6); The Fire from Within (Chapters 1, 5, 7, & 15) [92, 97–98, 104, 142–143]; The Power of Silence (Introduction).
What Crosses It: The entire physical body and luminous egg together.
Mechanism & Threshold: Reached when the glow of awareness kindles all the Eagle's emanations inside the human cocoon at once. This produces an instantaneous "burst of incandescence" where the boundaries of the shell dissolve.
Reversibility: Strictly Irreversible. The practitioner is consumed by "a fire from within," instantly converting their physical organism into pure awareness and vanishing from the earth. There is no returning to a mortal human life afterward; the individual enters "the definitive journey" into eternity.

5. The Moment a Warrior "Loses the Human Form"
Book Titles & Chapters: The Second Ring of Power (Chapter 2) [57–58]; The Eagle's Gift (Chapters 1 & 6); The Fire from Within (Chapter 8); The Active Side of Infinity (Chapter 12).
What Crosses It: The warrior's energetic alignment / assemblage point.
Mechanism & Threshold: The "human form" is described as the sticky, compelling force of alignment that binds awareness to a specific habitual position, enforcing human self-image and habits. When the assemblage point permanently drifts away from its original setting, the binding force breaks. In The Second Ring of Power, la Gorda describes this as a violent physical crisis (resembling a massive heart attack) where the sticky force leaves the body, after which an "eye" appears in dreaming [57–58].
Reversibility: Irreversible. Once the human form is shed, the warrior loses the capacity to be their old self or enjoy old habits [57–58, 74]. Don Juan states in The Fire from Within that it marks an "inexorable movement" resulting in the "irreversible loss of our affiliation to the force that makes us persons".
Cross-Book Disagreement/Shift: In The Second Ring of Power, la Gorda presents losing the human form as a dramatic bodily illness and an absolute state where "you are nothing" [57–58]. In The Fire from Within, don Juan re-conceptualizes it energetically as a simple drift of the assemblage point to the left. In The Active Side of Infinity, don Juan softens the absolute nature of the threshold, describing it as "the clear view" where human pettiness dissipates like fog, but explicitly warning that it is "not an accomplished fact" or permanent guarantee—a warrior must continuously maintain impeccability because "the fight will never end".
