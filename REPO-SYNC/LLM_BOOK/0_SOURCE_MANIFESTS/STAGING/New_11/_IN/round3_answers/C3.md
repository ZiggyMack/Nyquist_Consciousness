## 1. Candidate 1: The "Flyers' Mind" Running a Person (The Foreign Installation)
The Active Side of Infinity (Chapter 16 "Mud Shadows"). Don Juan reveals that cosmic predators called "flyers" consumed humanity's glowing coat of awareness down to a tiny fringe at the feet (the epicenter of self-reflection). In place of authentic human awareness, the flyers imposed their own mind—a "foreign installation" that is "baroque, contradictory, morose," and obsessed with self-pity and ego-defense [167, 172–174]. Under this control, human beings continue to build societies, hold jobs, converse, and behave with full outward competence, entirely unaware that their original authentic self ("the true mind") has been eaten to a stump and that their daily actions are generated by an alien predator [172–174, 185, 186]. Does the Match Hold? EXACT MATCH. The authentic self that generated human behavior has been devoured, while an alien control structure maintains outward behavioral competence without anything showing on the surface.

## 2. Candidate 2: "Phantom" People in the Second Attention
The Eagle's Gift (Chapters 1 & 3), The Fire from Within (Chapter 4), and The Art of Dreaming (Chapters 3 & 4) [137–138, 142, 288–289]. Inorganic beings and ancient sorcerers can project "phantoms" or phantasmagorical projections into both dreams and the daily world. These phantoms appear, move, speak, and interact with complete physical realism, looking like normal human beings on the outside. Inside them is no human organism, soul, or living spirit—they are hollow energy projections manipulated from behind the scenes. Does the Match Hold? STRONG MATCH: full outward competence and social presence while lacking an internal human self or spirit.

## 3. Candidate 3: The Person "Dead" to the Spirit / Living on Momentum
Journey to Ixtlan (Chapter 4), The Power of Silence (Chapter 1), and the YouTube abridged A Separate Reality. Don Juan describes average human beings whose "connecting link with intent is practically dead". They spend their lives repeating habitual routines—like a man planting corn year after year until he lies around like an old dog—acting out social scripts entirely on momentum. [The digest file uses our term "Walking Dead" in its comparative table for this.] Does the Match Hold? PARTIAL MATCH: these individuals behave competently on momentum and are spiritually "dead" to intent, but they still possess their own individual human awareness (trapped in self-reflection), rather than having their generating self completely destroyed or replaced.

## 4. Candidate 4: Sorcerers of Antiquity Who Became Trapped
The Fire from Within (Chapters 7 & 8) and The Art of Dreaming (Chapters 1 & 3) [146–147, 206–210, 221, 265]. Ancient sorcerers shifted their assemblage points outside their luminous eggs, transforming into "lines of energy" or taking up residence inside the inorganic realm ("the labyrinth of penumbra"). Does the Match Hold? FAILS. These ancient sorcerers did not collapse or become hollow walking dead. They stretched the duration of their consciousness and remain acutely alive, aware, and capricious to this day as energy lines or allies [147, 209–210, 265].

## 5. Candidate 5: The Drained or "Energetically Dead" Apprentice
The Second Ring of Power (Chapters 1 & 3) and The Art of Dreaming (Chapter 7) [169, 239–241]. When Castaneda is defeated by inorganic beings in dreaming, he returned to ordinary reality "energetically dead" or "gravely wounded". Does the Match Hold? FAILS. When a person's energy is drained or "dead" in the books, they do not continue functioning competently on the outside; they experience severe physical collapse, bedridden weakness, or incapacitation until their energy body is repaired.

## Shifts Across the Decades
Early/middle books (Journey to Ixtlan / Tales of Power): the mechanical, routine behavior of average human beings is attributed to social conditioning and internal dialogue. Final book (The Active Side of Infinity, Chapter 16): don Juan radically reframes this mechanical behavior as literal cosmic predation: the "flyers" ate human awareness and installed their own mind, turning humans into unwitting, competent cattle [172–174].
