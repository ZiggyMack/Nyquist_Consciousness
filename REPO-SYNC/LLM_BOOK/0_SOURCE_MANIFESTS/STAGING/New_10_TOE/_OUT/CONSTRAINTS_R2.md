Staged 2026-10-14. These sit on top of Round 1's constraints and the New_11 IP line, and they answer the "risky" Ziggy flagged when he pointed at this notebook. Three risks, three rules.

## 1. The speaker is a living person
- His **framework** may be drawn on, with attribution in the writer's files ("a real person's framework is used → attribution where used", the series plan's own rule).
- His **words** are not reproduced beyond short quotes (seven words or fewer), and no quote goes into a script or a prompt.
- He is **not a character**, not a model for one, and not named in any story-facing output. The notebook is told to say "the speaker" and "the second speaker" (the Q&A respondent), never names.
- The talk was given at a conference and is on his podcast; it is public, not private. That does not change the above.

## 2. The mind is Ziggy's and his friend's
- The character Angels is a character. Nothing about the real Angel (facts, history, voice, circumstances) enters any file. "Engineers talking shit" is a lyric and a disposition, not a biography.
- Ziggy's engineering lens enters the way it enters everything in this repo: as instruments and habits (the step response, settling time, ringback, the probe that loads the circuit it measures), by path to the Nyquist work, never as a portrait.

## 3. Operators, not sermons
- Every answer is asked for as **behavior**: what a person does, asks, builds, refuses, or stops doing. A reasoning move that cannot be shown without a speech is flagged, not scripted.
- No character explains the framework. If Lisan or Angels says a sentence that could sit in a lecture, the writer cuts it.
- The notebook is given the two-engineer frame (a young one with a hunch, an older one who tests everything, a watcher who refuses to build) and nothing else of the Lisan canon. No names, places, episodes, or lyrics go into the notebook.
