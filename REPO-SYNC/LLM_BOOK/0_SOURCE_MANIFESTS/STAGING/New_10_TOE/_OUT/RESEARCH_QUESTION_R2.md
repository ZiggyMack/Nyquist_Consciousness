# Research Question — New_10_TOE Round 2: the engineer's mind for Lisan

**Date staged:** 2026-10-14 · **Driver:** Nyquist repo Claude · **For:** the Lisan Al Gaib writer's pass (PanHandlers Claude, CFA)
**Ziggy's direction (2026-10-14, in substance):** "It's time we gave Lisan my engineering mind." The Meta Mirror line "Engineers talking shit" (Verse 2) is about Ziggy and his friend Angel, both electrical engineers; the series already has Angels (the older engineer who follows Lisan and does not believe the sign) and Episode 5 "The Rig" with that line as its cue. The TOE notebook (Curt Jaimungal's "The Reverse Elephant", Round 1 complete) is the well.

## The question

Round 1 extracted the speaker's operating system for thinking (every operation has a domain of validity; the four ways a problem gets "solved"; the local-to-global failure taxonomy; slogan fatigue; valid versus decorative compression; pre-articulate intimation; averted vision). Round 2 asks the same well a different question:

**How do those moves look as behavior, in a scene, when two engineers who like each other are arguing about something built from parts that each worked somewhere else?**

Three characters receive the answers, at idea level only:
- **Lisan**, the young engineer with the true hunch he cannot yet state (the generation corner: pre-articulate intimation, the detector that fires on the right articulation).
- **Angels**, the older engineer who tests everything and has heard the sign a thousand times (the composition and constraint corners: domain-of-validity testing, fiat detection, slogan fatigue as a method, not a mood).
- **Jet**, who refuses to build and refuses to explain (the one who knows which syntheses are blocked and says nothing until the gluing is earned).

## What a good round produces

- A catalog of the speaker's reasoning moves as **verb phrases with a physical tell** (what the person does, says, asks, or refuses when performing the move), so a writer can put them in a body without a speech.
- The local-to-global taxonomy applied to **a build from salvaged parts** (parts that worked in their original machines; a rig made from them), with the signs of each failure mode a mechanic could notice.
- The **fiat test** as a line an older engineer can say to a prophecy.
- The rules for **how a mentor treats an apprentice's hunch** without formalizing it for him.
- Where the young engineer and the old one **need each other and fail alone**.

## What this round is not

- Not a second audit of the sheaf formalism (Round 1 did that; the "candidate Phenomenon B/C" convention stands).
- Not a portrait of the speaker. He is a living person; his ideas are attributed where used, his words are not reproduced beyond short quotes, and he is not a character.
- Not a biography of the real Angel. The character Angels is a character; nothing about the real person enters the record.

## Lines carried from Round 1 that Round 2 must respect

- "Candidate Phenomenon B/C" until a formal obstruction certificate exists (Q34).
- The architecture lives in the transition data, not the nodes (Q33). For a rig: in how the parts join, not in the parts.
- Anti-premature unification, not anti-unification (Q37): only unify when the gluing conditions are earned.
- The Discovery Simplex corners (Transformation / Generation / Constraint / Composition) are an EOS hypothesis, not the speaker's claim.
