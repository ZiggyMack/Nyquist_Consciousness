## (c) The Two Failure Modes
**Articulating Too Early (Premature Formalization):**
- Mechanism: The investigator rushes to fit the hunch into a rigid, explicit model. This triggers the "definitional inversion problem" (Chapter 7: Averted Vision and Consciousness), where the explicit formal model comes to be "worshipped" as God (Chapter 7: Averted Vision and Consciousness), devaluing the original intuition and destroying the phenomenon through deflation (Chapter 7: Averted Vision and Consciousness, Chapter 9: Pre-articulate Scientific Intimations).

**Never Articulating (Perpetual Vagueness):**
- Mechanism: The investigator remains perpetually in the cloud without ever attempting to dredge up a solid rock or test the idea against formal tools (Chapter 11: Ontological Significance of Vagueness). The speaker notes that formalization is necessary because mathematical tools like sheaf theory must be able to "come back with theorems" (Chapter 11: Ontological Significance of Vagueness) that inform and eliminate philosophical problems (Chapter 11: Ontological Significance of Vagueness).
